Keeping aircraft position trustworthy when GPS is jammed or faked

A low-orbit (LEO) navigation layer over India, used together with GPS and India's NavIC, plus an onboard check that spots faked GPS and corrects the position. Architecture with back-of-envelope physics and cost, and a simulation of the AI correction.

Architecture

1 · LEO layer114 smallsats at 1000 km broadcast ranging signals plus correction and authentication messages over Indian airspace.
2 · ReceiverAircraft use GPS, NavIC and LEO together; three independent sources are hard to fake at once.
3 · Onboard checkA small model compares the constellations every second; if GPS disagrees, it is dropped and the fix comes from NavIC + LEO.
4 · Insights downSatellites and aircraft report "GPS faked here" events, not raw signals, to NavContinuity's GNSS map and passenger recovery.

Jamming, to scale physics

Free-space link budget at L1 (1575.42 MHz), 10° elevation mask. GPS arrives at -128.5 dBm (spec minimum); a LEO satellite at 1000 km with the same transmit power arrives 19 dB stronger. Jammer: 10 W, receiver loses lock above 30 dB jammer-to-signal (assumed). Line-of-sight; terrain and antennas change real ranges.

  • With GPS alone, a consistent spoof is invisible: both checks catch about 1%.
  • Adding independent constellations is what makes it detectable. Adding LEO lowers the corrected error from 19 m to 8 m.
  • Honest result: our simple AI check (logistic regression) catches slightly fewer spoofs than the classic chi-square test (92% vs 96%). The gain comes from the extra signals; a stronger model is future work.

Who pays, and what it's worth

Buyers: the Airports Authority of India and DGCA (national resilience, as GAGAN was funded), airlines through a per-flight integrity subscription, and defence and critical-infrastructure users. Worth: India reported 2,354 GPS interference incidents from Nov 2023 to Dec 2025; one commercial diversion costs €1,000–77,200 (EUROCONTROL, 2022 prices). If the layer prevented even 1 in 10 interference events from turning into a diversion, the avoided cost would be weighed against the $82M a year (illustrative, not measured).
