Where GPS is being jammed or degraded over India

Aircraft constantly broadcast how much they trust their own GPS position (the NIC value in ADS-B). When many aircraft in one area report low trust at once, GPS there is being jammed or degraded. Real data from 72 snapshots on 2026-11-01. Monitoring only: no transmitter hunting, no tracking of individual flights.

Method and limits

  • Source: ADS-B Exchange free sample data (readsb-hist), aircraft-reported NIC. 72 global snapshots, filtered to 5–38°N, 60–98°E.
  • An aircraft counts as degraded in a 1° area when most of its reports there have NIC below 7. Areas are rated by the share of degraded aircraft, the approach used by public maps such as gpsjam.org.
  • NIC drops can also come from avionics faults or single aircraft; ratings need several aircraft per cell.
  • Sampled snapshots, not continuous coverage.
  • Shows where GPS was degraded, not who caused it (protective use only).
  • Who pays: airlines and airports (dispatch planning), DGCA and AAI (national monitoring), insurers. Worth: India logged 2,354 interference reports Nov 2023–Dec 2025; each avoided diversion saves €1,000–77,200 (EUROCONTROL, 2022 prices).
